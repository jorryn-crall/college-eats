Cheap Meals That Work for a College Budget

Eating on a budget does not mean eating the same boring dinner every night. With affordable pantry staples and a few fresh ingredients, you can make filling meals without spending a fortune.

These cheap college meals use simple ingredients and everyday cooking methods. Estimated prices are illustrative and may vary by store and location.

4 Affordable Meals to Try

1. Cheesy Tomato Pasta

Estimated cost: $1.50 per serving

Time: 20 minutes · Serves 2

Ingredients

  • 4 ounces dry pasta
  • 1 cup canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1/4 cup shredded cheese
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning
  • Salt and pepper

Directions

  1. Cook pasta according to the package directions.
  2. Warm the tomatoes and seasoning in a pan.
  3. Mix in the drained pasta and cheese. Stir until combined and serve.

2. Egg and Rice Bowl

Estimated cost: $1.75 per serving

Time: 15 minutes · Serves 1

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cooked rice
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup frozen mixed vegetables
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon cooking oil

Directions

  1. Heat the oil in a skillet and cook the vegetables until hot.
  2. Add the rice and soy sauce and stir.
  3. Cook the eggs in the skillet, scramble, and mix everything together.

3. Loaded Baked Potato

Estimated cost: $1.50 per serving

Time: 10–15 minutes in a microwave · Serves 1

Ingredients

  • 1 medium russet potato
  • 1/4 cup shredded cheese
  • 2 tablespoons plain Greek yogurt
  • 2 tablespoons sliced green onion, optional
  • Salt and pepper

Directions

  1. Wash the potato and pierce it several times with a fork.
  2. Microwave on a microwave-safe plate, turning halfway through, until soft.
  3. Split the potato, add cheese and yogurt, and season to taste.

4. Chickpea Tomato Skillet

Estimated cost: $2.00 per serving

Time: 15 minutes · Serves 2

Ingredients

  • 1 can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup canned diced tomatoes
  • 1 cup fresh or frozen spinach
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika

Directions

  1. Add tomatoes, chickpeas, garlic powder, and paprika to a skillet.
  2. Simmer for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  3. Stir in spinach until wilted and serve on its own or over rice.

How to Save Money on College Groceries

  • Build meals around staples. Rice, oats, pasta, potatoes, and eggs can be used in several recipes.
  • Buy frozen produce. Frozen vegetables and fruit are convenient, last longer, and help reduce food waste.
  • Reuse ingredients. Buy ingredients that work in more than one meal instead of purchasing something new for every recipe.
  • Compare unit prices. Check the cost per ounce or pound to compare package sizes.
  • Plan before shopping. Check your pantry and refrigerator first so you do not buy duplicates.
Budget challenge: Try planning five dinners around a short grocery list. Choose two or three overlapping ingredients and use pantry staples you already own to fill in the gaps.
